What to Expect at a Greece CSD CSE Meeting

A CSE meeting in Greece CSD typically follows an agenda that includes reviewing your child's progress, discussing evaluation results, and developing or revising the Individualized Education Program (IEP). Key participants include:

  • You: As the parent, you are a critical member of the team.
  • Special Education Teacher: Provides expertise on special education strategies.
  • General Education Teacher: Offers insights into the general education curriculum.
  • School Psychologist: Interprets evaluations and offers recommendations.
  • District Representative: Ensures the IEP aligns with district resources.

Your role is to share your observations, ask questions, and advocate for your child's needs. Be prepared to discuss your child's strengths, weaknesses, and goals.

How to Prepare for Your CSE Meeting

Effective preparation is key. Request the following documents at least 5 school days before the meeting:

  1. Draft IEP (if available)
  2. Evaluation reports (psychological, educational, etc.)
  3. Progress reports from teachers and therapists

Remember that New York State mandates evaluations be completed within 60 school days of parental consent. If evaluations are delayed, address this concern at the CSE meeting. Review all documents carefully and note any questions or concerns. Consider bringing any private evaluation reports or relevant documentation about your child.

Your Rights During the CSE Meeting

You have specific rights as a parent during the CSE meeting:

  • Right to Disagree: You can disagree with any part of the IEP. Your concerns must be documented.
  • Right to Adjournment: You can request to postpone the meeting for more time to review information or seek outside consultation.
  • Right to Representation: You can bring anyone to the meeting, including an advocate, lawyer, or therapist.

Be assertive in expressing your opinions and advocating for your child. Don't hesitate to ask for clarification or challenge recommendations you believe are not in your child's best interest.

After the CSE Meeting: Next Steps

After the meeting, you will receive Prior Written Notice, which summarizes the decisions made and explains the district's rationale. Review this document carefully.

You have the right to reject the IEP and request mediation or file a complaint with the New York State Education Department. If you agree with the IEP, the school must implement it within 10 school days. Follow up with your child's teachers and therapists to ensure the IEP is being implemented correctly. Schedule regular check-ins with the special education teacher to monitor progress.

Before Your CSE Meeting — Checklist

  • Request all relevant documents at least 5 days before the meeting.
  • Review your child's evaluation reports and progress reports.
  • Write down your questions and concerns about the IEP.
  • Consider bringing an advocate or support person to the meeting.
  • Prepare to share your observations about your child's needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Greece CSD parents ask

What is the difference between CPSE and CSE in New York?

In New York, the Committee on Preschool Special Education (CPSE) serves children ages 3-5. The Committee on Special Education (CSE) serves children ages 5-21. So, if your child is transitioning from preschool to kindergarten in Greece CSD, they will move from CPSE to CSE.

What happens if I disagree with the IEP developed by the CSE at Greece CSD?

If you disagree with the IEP, you have several options, including requesting mediation, filing a state complaint, or requesting an impartial hearing. The district must provide you with information about these options. It's crucial to document your concerns and communicate them clearly to the CSE.

How long does Greece CSD have to complete a special education evaluation?

According to New York State regulations (Part 200), the district has 60 school days from the date you provide consent for evaluation to complete the evaluation and hold a CSE meeting to discuss the results and develop an IEP, if necessary.
