Greece CSD

Discipline Rights & Impartial Hearings
in Greece CSD

When Greece Central School District disciplines your special education student, it must follow specific procedures to protect their rights.

Federal law provides special education students with discipline protections that Greece CSD must follow. These protections are triggered when a student is removed from their current placement for more than 10 school days.

The 10-Day Rule in Greece CSD

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), Greece CSD must hold a Manifestation Determination Review (MDR) if a student with an IEP is:

  • Removed from their current placement for more than 10 consecutive school days, or
  • Subject to a series of removals that constitute a pattern, even if each individual removal is less than 10 days. Factors considered in determining a pattern include the length of each removal, the total amount of time the student is removed, and the proximity of the removals to each other.

The MDR must be convened no later than the tenth day of the suspension.

What Happens at a Manifestation Determination Review (MDR)?

At the MDR, the Committee on Special Education (CSE) must determine if the student's behavior was a manifestation of their disability. The CSE must answer both of these questions:

  1. Was the conduct in question caused by, or did it have a direct and substantial relationship to, the child's disability?
  2. Was the conduct in question the direct result of the Greece CSD’s failure to implement the IEP?

Evidence considered includes the student's IEP, teacher observations, and any relevant information provided by the parents.

If the Behavior IS a Manifestation of the Disability

If the CSE determines that the behavior was a manifestation of the student's disability, the district must take immediate steps to address the behavior. This includes:

  • Conducting a functional behavioral assessment (FBA), unless one has already been conducted.
  • Implementing a behavior intervention plan (BIP) for the student, or revising the existing BIP if one is already in place.
  • Returning the student to their placement unless the parents and Greece CSD agree to a change of placement as part of the IEP team process.

Challenging Disciplinary Actions Through Impartial Hearings in New York

In New York State, parents have the right to challenge disciplinary actions by requesting an impartial hearing. This is a legal proceeding where an impartial hearing officer (IHO) hears evidence and makes a decision about the appropriateness of the disciplinary action. Parents can challenge a disciplinary placement if they believe it violates their child's rights under IDEA or Part 200 of the Regulations of the Commissioner of Education.

To request an impartial hearing, parents must file a written request with the district. There are strict timelines for filing a request and for the hearing process itself. It is highly recommended to seek legal advice from a special education attorney or advocate familiar with New York's impartial hearing process.

When to Get Legal Help Immediately

  • Repeated suspensions without MDRs
  • Refusal to conduct an FBA after behavioral incidents
  • Failure to implement the BIP
  • Pressure to agree to a change of placement

Frequently Asked Questions

Greece CSD parents ask

What happens if the CSE determines the behavior was NOT a manifestation of my child's disability?

If the CSE determines that the behavior was not a manifestation of your child's disability, Greece CSD can discipline your child in the same way it disciplines students without disabilities. However, the district must still provide your child with a free and appropriate public education (FAPE) during the disciplinary period. This may involve providing services in an interim alternative educational setting (IAES).

What is an Interim Alternative Educational Setting (IAES)?

An IAES is a temporary educational placement for students who have been suspended or expelled for disciplinary reasons. The IAES must be designed to allow the student to continue to participate in the general education curriculum, although in another setting, and to continue to receive services and modifications outlined in the IEP so as to enable them to meet the goals set out in the IEP.

How do I file a complaint with NYSED if I believe Greece CSD violated my child's rights?

You can file a formal complaint with the New York State Education Department (NYSED) if you believe Greece CSD violated your child's rights under IDEA. The complaint must be in writing and must describe the violation, provide relevant facts, and propose a resolution. You can find more information about the NYSED complaint process on the NYSED website or by contacting your local Parent Training and Information Center.
