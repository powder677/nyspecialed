In New York State, school districts have 60 school days to complete an evaluation after receiving parental consent. You also have the right to request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) if you disagree with the district's evaluation.

How to Request a District Evaluation in Greece CSD

To request a special education evaluation for your child in Greece Central School District, you must submit a written request to the school principal or the district's special education office.

Your request should include:

  • Your child's full name and date of birth
  • Your child's school and grade
  • A clear statement that you are requesting a special education evaluation to determine eligibility for special education services.
  • A brief description of your concerns about your child's academic, social, emotional, or behavioral development.
  • Your contact information, including phone number and email address.

Example: "I am writing to request a special education evaluation for my child, [Child's Name], who is in [Grade] at [School Name]. I am concerned about [Child's Specific Concerns]."

NY State Evaluation Timeline: The 60 School Day Rule

New York State regulations require school districts to complete the initial evaluation process within 60 school days of receiving parental consent. This timeline includes:

  1. Obtaining parental consent for the evaluation.
  2. Conducting the necessary assessments and observations.
  3. Holding a meeting of the Committee on Special Education (CSE) to review the evaluation results and determine eligibility.

If the district fails to meet the 60-day timeline, you have the right to file a complaint with the New York State Education Department. Document all communication with Greece CSD related to the evaluation request and timeline.

Your Right to an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at District Expense

If you disagree with the results of the evaluation conducted by Greece CSD, you have the right to obtain an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense. This means that the district is responsible for paying for the IEE.

  • You must first request the IEE in writing.
  • The district may either agree to pay for the IEE or initiate a due process hearing to demonstrate that its evaluation was appropriate.
  • If the district initiates a hearing and the hearing officer determines that the district's evaluation was appropriate, you still have the right to obtain an IEE, but you will have to pay for it yourself.
  • The results of any IEE must be considered by the CSE when making decisions about your child's special education program.

Types of Evaluations: Neuropsych, Speech, OT, PT, FBA

Depending on your child's needs, different types of evaluations may be necessary. Some common types of evaluations include:

  • Neuropsychological Evaluation: Assesses cognitive functioning, learning disabilities, and other neurological issues.
  • Speech and Language Evaluation: Evaluates speech clarity, language comprehension, and communication skills.
  • Occupational Therapy (OT) Evaluation: Assesses fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living skills.
  • Physical Therapy (PT) Evaluation: Evaluates gross motor skills, balance, and coordination.
  • Functional Behavioral Assessment (FBA): Identifies the triggers and functions of challenging behaviors.

When requesting an evaluation, be specific about the areas of concern and request the evaluations that are most relevant to your child's needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

Greece CSD parents ask

What happens if Greece CSD doesn't complete the evaluation within 60 school days?

If the Greece CSD fails to complete the initial evaluation process within the required 60 school days, you have the right to file a complaint with the New York State Education Department. You can also request mediation or pursue other legal remedies to ensure your child receives a timely evaluation.

How do I request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) from Greece CSD?

To request an IEE, you must submit a written request to the Greece CSD's special education department. The district then has the option to either fund the IEE or file for a due process hearing to defend the appropriateness of their own evaluation. It is important to keep records of all communication.

What should I do if I disagree with the CSE's recommendations after the evaluation?

If you disagree with the Committee on Special Education (CSE) recommendations following the evaluation in Greece CSD, you have several options. These include requesting mediation, filing a complaint with the New York State Education Department, or pursuing due process. Consulting with a special education advocate can provide guidance on navigating these options.
