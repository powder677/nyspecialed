What Is an IEP in NYC District 25 - Flushing / Whitestone?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services and supports your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has a disability that affects their learning, an IEP ensures they receive the personalized education they deserve at no cost to your family. This powerful tool transforms your child's educational experience by providing tailored goals, specialized instruction, and necessary accommodations.

For families in NYC District 25's diverse Flushing and Whitestone communities, understanding the IEP process is crucial for advocating effectively for your child. Whether you're navigating this process in English, Korean, Mandarin, Spanish, or another language spoken in our neighborhood, you have the right to fully participate in your child's educational planning. The IEP process can feel overwhelming, but knowing your rights and the steps involved empowers you to be your child's strongest advocate.

The IEP Process in NYC District 25 - Flushing / Whitestone

The IEP process in District 25 follows NYC Department of Education procedures while serving our culturally diverse community. The Committee on Special Education (CSE) oversees this process, ensuring each child receives appropriate evaluations and services.

Step 1: Initial Request or Referral - You, your child's teacher, or school staff can request a special education evaluation. In District 25's multilingual community, interpretation services are available throughout this process to ensure all families can participate fully.

Step 2: Evaluation and Assessment - Your child will receive comprehensive evaluations to determine if they have a disability and need special education services. These assessments consider your child's cultural and linguistic background.

Step 3: CSE Meeting - The Committee on Special Education meets to review evaluation results and, if appropriate, develop an IEP. This meeting includes you as an equal team member, along with school professionals.

Step 4: IEP Implementation - Once you consent to the IEP, your child begins receiving the specified services and supports in their school setting.

Step 5: Annual Review - The CSE reviews and updates your child's IEP at least once yearly, with ongoing monitoring throughout the school year.

Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

• You (the parent/guardian) - Your input is essential and legally required • Special education teacher - Provides expertise in specialized instruction • General education teacher - Offers perspective on grade-level curriculum • School psychologist or CSE representative - Interprets evaluation results • Related service providers - Such as speech therapists or occupational therapists, if applicable • Your child - When appropriate, especially for older students • District representative - Ensures resources and services can be provided

As a parent, you have the right to bring advocates, request interpreters, and invite anyone with knowledge or special expertise about your child to attend the meeting.

Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 25 - Flushing / Whitestone

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State law, you have comprehensive rights throughout the special education process:

Prior Written Notice - You must receive written notice before any changes to your child's identification, evaluation, or placement, provided in your native language when possible.

Consent Rights - Schools cannot evaluate your child or begin providing special education services without your informed written consent.

Participation in Decision-Making - You are an equal member of the CSE team with the right to participate in all decisions about your child's education.

Access to Records - You can review all educational records related to your child's evaluation, IEP, and placement.

Independent Educational Evaluation - If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an independent evaluation at public expense.

Due Process Rights - You have the right to mediation, impartial hearings, and appeals if you disagree with CSE decisions.

Language Rights - All meetings, documents, and communications should be provided in your native language or preferred mode of communication.

Common IEP Services Available in NYC

NYC District 25 schools can provide various special education services and supports:

• Special Education Teacher Support Services (SETSS) - Direct instruction from a special education teacher in small groups or individual sessions • Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) Classes - General and special education teachers work together in the same classroom • Speech-Language Therapy - Addresses communication, language, and speech difficulties • Occupational Therapy (OT) - Helps develop fine motor skills and daily living abilities • Physical Therapy (PT) - Improves gross motor skills, strength, and mobility • Counseling Services - Provides emotional and behavioral support • Assistive Technology - Devices and software to support learning and communication • Modified Instructional Services (MIS) - Specialized programs for students with significant cognitive disabilities

Next Steps for Parents

  1. Document your concerns - Keep detailed records of your child's academic, behavioral, or developmental challenges that suggest they may need special education support.

  2. Request an evaluation in writing - Submit a written request to your child's school principal and District 25's Committee on Special Education for a comprehensive evaluation.

  3. Prepare for the CSE meeting - Gather relevant documents, prepare questions, and consider bringing an advocate or support person to help you navigate the process.

  4. Review all documents carefully - Take time to understand evaluation reports and proposed IEP recommendations before signing any agreements.

  5. Stay engaged throughout implementation - Regularly communicate with your child's teachers and service providers to monitor progress and address any concerns.
