What Is an IEP in NYC District 22 - Flatbush / Marine Park?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines specialized instruction and services for students with disabilities in NYC District 22, which serves the diverse Flatbush and Marine Park communities in Brooklyn. This comprehensive plan ensures your child receives the support they need to succeed academically, socially, and emotionally in school.

For families in District 22's vibrant multicultural neighborhoods—from the Caribbean communities along Church Avenue to the established Jewish enclaves near Avenue J—understanding your child's IEP rights is crucial. The IEP serves as your child's educational roadmap, detailing specific goals, accommodations, and services that must be provided at no cost to your family, regardless of your immigration status or primary language spoken at home.

The IEP Process in NYC District 22 - Flatbush / Marine Park

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in NYC District 22 follows the same federal and state guidelines as all NYC districts, but understanding the local context can help you navigate more effectively. District 22 serves a remarkably diverse population, with families speaking Haitian Creole, Russian, Yiddish, Bengali, Spanish, and many other languages alongside English.

The process begins when you or your child's school suspects a disability may be impacting learning. You can request an evaluation at any time by writing to your school's principal or the District 22 CSE office. The evaluation phase typically takes 60 school days and includes assessments by school psychologists, social workers, and other specialists as needed.

Once evaluations are complete, the CSE team meets to determine if your child qualifies for special education services under one of 13 disability categories recognized by IDEA. If eligible, the team develops an IEP that includes annual goals, present levels of performance, and specific services. In District 22, these meetings often require interpretation services given the community's linguistic diversity, which the district must provide free of charge.

The IEP must be reviewed annually and your child re-evaluated at least every three years. However, you can request changes or additional meetings whenever you feel your child's needs aren't being met. District 22 has experienced CSE teams familiar with the unique challenges facing immigrant families and those navigating language barriers.

Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

• General education teacher - represents typical classroom expectations • Special education teacher - provides expertise in specialized instruction • District representative - can authorize services and commit district resources • School psychologist or evaluation specialist - interprets assessment results • Related service providers - such as speech therapists or occupational therapists (when applicable) • Parent or guardian - you are an equal member of this team with full participation rights

As a parent, you have the right to bring advocates, request interpreters, and invite anyone with knowledge of your child to attend the meeting.

Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 22 - Flatbush / Marine Park

Under IDEA and New York State Education Law, you have extensive rights that protect your child's access to appropriate special education services. You have the right to participate meaningfully in all IEP decisions, receive documents in your native language, and request independent evaluations if you disagree with the school's assessment results.

NYC provides additional protections including the right to an impartial hearing if disputes arise, access to free or low-cost legal advocacy through organizations, and protection from retaliation for advocating for your child. The district must provide prior written notice before making any changes to your child's IEP, giving you time to review and respond to proposed modifications.

You also have the right to access your child's educational records, observe your child in their classroom setting, and request IEP meetings whenever you believe changes are needed. These rights remain the same regardless of your immigration status, and District 22 cannot ask about or report on family immigration status when providing special education services.

Common IEP Services Available in NYC

NYC offers comprehensive special education services that may be included in your child's IEP:

• Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) Classes - general and special education teachers work together in inclusive classrooms • Special Class - smaller classes designed specifically for students with disabilities
• Related Services - speech therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, and counseling • SETSS (Special Education Teacher Support Services) - individualized or small group instruction • Paraprofessional Support - one-on-one or shared aide assistance • Assistive Technology - devices and software to support learning and communication • Transportation Services - specialized busing when needed for school access • Extended School Year (ESY) - summer programming to prevent significant regression

Next Steps for Parents

  1. Document your concerns - Keep written records of your child's struggles, including specific examples of academic, behavioral, or social difficulties you've observed.

  2. Request an evaluation in writing - Submit a formal letter to your school's principal and keep a copy for your records.

  3. Prepare for meetings - Gather report cards, work samples, and any outside evaluations or medical records that support your concerns.

  4. Learn the terminology - Familiarize yourself with special education acronyms and legal terms to participate more effectively in discussions.

  5. Connect with other parents - Join local parent support groups or online communities specific to District 22 to learn from others' experiences.
