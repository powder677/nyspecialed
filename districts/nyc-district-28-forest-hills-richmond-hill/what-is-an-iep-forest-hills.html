What Is an IEP in NYC District 28 - Forest Hills / Richmond Hill?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services, supports, and goals for students with disabilities. In NYC District 28, which serves the diverse communities of Forest Hills, Richmond Hill, Kew Gardens, and surrounding areas, IEPs are developed through the Committee on Special Education (CSE) process to ensure every child receives a free appropriate public education (FAPE) tailored to their unique needs.

If you're a parent in District 28 navigating the special education system, understanding your rights and the IEP process is crucial. This culturally rich district, home to families from South Asia, Latin America, and many other backgrounds, recognizes that effective special education must honor each family's language, culture, and values while providing the highest quality services to help children thrive academically, socially, and emotionally.

The IEP Process in NYC District 28 - Forest Hills / Richmond Hill

The CSE process in NYC District 28 follows the same framework as all NYC districts, but local schools work to accommodate the district's multilingual community, with translation services available in languages including Spanish, Hindi, Bengali, Korean, and others commonly spoken in Forest Hills and Richmond Hill.

Step 1: Initial Referral - Parents, teachers, or other professionals can refer a student for special education evaluation. Referrals can be made in your native language, and the district must provide interpretation services.

Step 2: Evaluation - The CSE conducts comprehensive evaluations to determine if the child has a disability and needs special education services. Evaluations consider cultural and linguistic factors relevant to District 28's diverse population.

Step 3: Eligibility Determination - The CSE team reviews evaluation results to determine eligibility for special education services under one of 13 disability categories.

Step 4: IEP Development - If eligible, the CSE team develops an individualized education program with specific goals, services, and placement recommendations.

Step 5: Implementation - The IEP is implemented in the least restrictive environment appropriate for the child, often within District 28 schools or other NYC DOE programs.

Step 6: Annual Review - IEPs are reviewed and updated annually, with ongoing progress monitoring throughout the school year.

Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

• A special education teacher • A general education teacher (when appropriate) • A school psychologist or other qualified evaluator • A district representative who can authorize services • Related services providers (as needed) • The student (when appropriate, typically age 14+) • Parents/guardians

Parents have the right to bring advocates, interpreters, or other support persons to IEP meetings, and all meetings must be conducted in your preferred language.

Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 28 - Forest Hills / Richmond Hill

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, parents in District 28 have comprehensive rights including:

• Prior Written Notice - The right to receive written notice before any changes to your child's identification, evaluation, or placement • Consent Rights - Your written consent is required before initial evaluations and before first-time special education services • Language Access - All IEP documents and meetings must be provided in your native language • Independent Educational Evaluation - The right to obtain an independent evaluation at public expense if you disagree with the district's evaluation • Due Process Rights - Access to mediation, due process hearings, and appeals if disputes arise • Records Access - The right to inspect and copy your child's educational records • Participation - Meaningful participation in all IEP decisions affecting your child

Common IEP Services Available in NYC

Special Education Teacher Support Services (SETSS) - Direct instruction from certified special education teachers in small group or individual settings

Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) - General education classrooms with both a general education and special education teacher serving students with and without disabilities

Speech-Language Therapy - Services to address communication disorders, language development, and speech articulation

Occupational Therapy (OT) - Support for fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living skills needed for school success

Physical Therapy (PT) - Services to address gross motor skills, mobility, and physical development

Counseling Services - Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional needs and behavioral goals

Related Services - Including vision/hearing services, assistive technology, transportation, and nursing services

Specialized Programs - District 75 programs for students with significant disabilities requiring highly specialized instruction

Next Steps for Parents

  1. Document your concerns - Keep detailed records of your child's academic, behavioral, or developmental challenges that may indicate a need for special education services.

  2. Request an evaluation in writing - Submit a formal written request to your child's school or District 28's CSE office for a special education evaluation.

  3. Gather supporting documentation - Collect medical records, previous evaluations, report cards, and teacher observations that support your request.

  4. Prepare for meetings - Review your rights, prepare questions, and consider bringing an advocate or support person to IEP meetings.

  5. Stay engaged throughout the process - Maintain regular communication with your child's IEP team and monitor progress toward goals.
