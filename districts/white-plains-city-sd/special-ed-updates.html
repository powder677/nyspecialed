What's Happening in White Plains City SD Special Education Right Now

White Plains City School District, like many districts in Westchester County, is currently facing increased scrutiny regarding the timely provision of special education services. A recent surge in student enrollment coupled with ongoing staffing shortages, particularly in the areas of speech-language pathology and school psychology, is placing a strain on the district's ability to conduct initial evaluations and re-evaluations within the mandated 60-day timeframe outlined in New York State Education Department (NYSED) regulations. This has led to an uptick in parental complaints and requests for independent educational evaluations (IEEs) at public expense.

Another area of concern involves the implementation of Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) within the least restrictive environment (LRE). Data indicates that a disproportionate number of students with disabilities in White Plains are being placed in more restrictive settings than necessary. Advocates are urging the district to prioritize inclusive practices, provide teachers with adequate professional development on differentiated instruction, and ensure that general education classrooms are equipped to support the diverse learning needs of all students. Concerns have also been raised about the fidelity of IEP implementation, with some parents reporting inconsistencies between the services outlined in their child's IEP and what is actually being delivered in the classroom.

White Plains City SD is also undergoing a review of its CSE procedures following several Impartial Hearing decisions that found the district to be in non-compliance with Part 200 Regulations. These decisions highlighted issues related to the adequacy of IEP goals, the appropriateness of behavioral interventions, and the insufficient consideration of parent input during the IEP development process. The district is now working to develop a corrective action plan to address these deficiencies and ensure that all students with disabilities receive a free and appropriate public education (FAPE) in accordance with state and federal law.

IEP & CSE Timeline Red Flags in NY State

Under NY State law (Part 200 Regulations), once you sign consent for an initial evaluation, the district has 60 calendar days to evaluate your child and hold the Committee on Special Education (CSE) meeting to determine eligibility. These timelines are legally binding.

One Action Step You Can Take Today

Send a written, dated request via email to your campus principal AND the White Plains City SD Special Education Office requesting a Full Evaluation if you have concerns. Verbal requests do not start the legal clock in New York.

Frequently Asked Questions: White Plains City SD Special Education

Real questions New York parents are searching for — answered with NYSED regulations in mind.

How do I request a special education evaluation for my child in White Plains City School District?

To request a special education evaluation, submit a written request to the school principal or the Special Education Department. The district then has 30 school days to respond to your request and either consent to the evaluation or deny it. If they consent, the evaluation must be completed within 60 days of your consent.

What is an IEP and how is it developed in White Plains City SD?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a written plan that outlines the special education and related services your child will receive. It is developed by the Committee on Special Education (CSE), which includes you, your child's teachers, a school psychologist, and a district representative. The IEP is based on your child's unique needs and goals.

What are my rights if I disagree with the IEP developed by the CSE?

If you disagree with the IEP, you have the right to request mediation, file a complaint with the New York State Education Department (NYSED), or request an Impartial Hearing. An Impartial Hearing is a formal legal proceeding where an independent hearing officer will review the IEP and make a decision.

How often is an IEP reviewed in White Plains City School District?

An IEP must be reviewed at least annually by the CSE. However, you can request a review more frequently if you believe your child's needs have changed. You should submit a written request to the school principal or the Special Education Department outlining your concerns.

What is the process for obtaining an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense in White Plains City SD?

If you disagree with the results of the district's evaluation, you have the right to request an IEE at public expense. You must notify the district of your disagreement and request. The district then has the option to either agree to pay for the IEE or initiate an Impartial Hearing to demonstrate that their evaluation was appropriate. If the hearing officer determines the district's evaluation was appropriate, you still have the right to obtain an IEE, but you will have to pay for it yourself.

What related services are available to students with IEPs in White Plains City School District?

Related services may include speech therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, counseling, transportation, and assistive technology. The specific related services your child receives will be determined by the CSE based on their individual needs.

How can I access my child’s special education records in White Plains City SD?

You have the right to access your child's special education records. Submit a written request to the school principal or the Special Education Department. The district must provide you with access to the records within a reasonable timeframe.
