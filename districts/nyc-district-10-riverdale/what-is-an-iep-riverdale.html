What Is an IEP in NYC District 10 - Fordham / Riverdale?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services your child will receive to succeed in school. If your child has been identified with a disability that impacts their learning, an IEP ensures they get the personalized support they need to reach their full potential. This isn't just paperwork—it's your child's roadmap to academic success and your family's guarantee that the school will provide appropriate services.

For families in NYC District 10, which serves the diverse communities of Fordham, University Heights, Morris Heights, Mount Eden, and Riverdale, understanding the IEP process is especially important. Our district's rich multicultural landscape means families speak many languages and come from various backgrounds, and every child deserves an education plan that respects their unique needs and cultural context while providing world-class special education services.

The IEP Process in NYC District 10 - Fordham / Riverdale

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in NYC District 10 follows the same legal framework as all NYC districts, but serves families across economically and culturally diverse neighborhoods—from the affluent Riverdale area to the working-class communities of Fordham and the South Bronx. Here's how the process works:

  1. Initial Referral : A parent, teacher, or other professional requests an evaluation for special education services
  2. Consent and Evaluation : The district must obtain written parental consent and complete a comprehensive evaluation within 60 school days
  3. CSE Meeting : The Committee convenes to review evaluation results and determine eligibility
  4. IEP Development : If eligible, the team creates an individualized education program tailored to your child's needs
  5. Implementation : Services begin, and progress is monitored throughout the school year
  6. Annual Review : The IEP is reviewed and updated at least once per year

District 10's diverse community means CSE meetings often include translation services for families who speak Spanish, Arabic, Bengali, and other languages prevalent in our neighborhoods. The district recognizes that effective communication with families is essential for developing meaningful IEPs.

Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

  • Parent or guardian (that's you!)
  • Special education teacher
  • General education teacher (if child participates in general education)
  • District representative (school psychologist or administrator)
  • Individual who can interpret evaluation results
  • Related service providers (speech therapist, occupational therapist, etc., as needed)
  • Student (when age-appropriate, typically 14+)

As a parent, you have the right to bring advocates, request interpreters, and invite anyone who has knowledge or special expertise about your child.

Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 10 - Fordham / Riverdale

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have significant rights:

  • Prior written notice before any changes to your child's identification, evaluation, or placement
  • Consent rights for initial evaluations and services
  • Access to records and the right to obtain independent evaluations
  • Due process protections if you disagree with the district's decisions
  • Language rights including interpretation and translation services
  • Participation rights as an equal member of the CSE team

NYC provides additional protections through the Office of Student Enrollment and Planning, and District 10 families can access parent coordinators who understand the unique challenges facing Bronx communities.

Common IEP Services Available in NYC

  • Speech-Language Therapy : Addresses communication, language processing, and speech articulation challenges
  • Occupational Therapy (OT) : Helps with fine motor skills, sensory processing, and activities of daily living
  • Physical Therapy (PT) : Supports gross motor development, mobility, and physical strength
  • Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) : General and special education teachers work together in the same classroom
  • Special Class : Smaller class sizes with specialized instruction for students with more intensive needs
  • Counseling Services : Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional and behavioral needs
  • Assistive Technology : Devices and software to support learning and communication
  • Related Services : Transportation, school health services, and other supports as needed

Next Steps for Parents

  1. Document your concerns by keeping records of your child's academic and behavioral challenges
  2. Request an evaluation in writing to your child's school principal and send a copy to the CSE office
  3. Prepare for meetings by reviewing your child's current performance and thinking about their needs
  4. Understand the timeline and follow up to ensure evaluations happen within required timeframes
  5. Connect with resources including parent training centers and District 10's family engagement coordinators
