How to Find Current Contacts in Rochester City SD

Navigating the Rochester City School District (RCSD) special education system starts with finding the right contacts. Because staff and roles can change frequently, it's essential to verify information directly on the district's official website. This ensures you have the most up-to-date information. The Rochester City School District website is: [https://www.rcsdk12.org/](https://www.rcsdk12.org/). Look for a "Departments" or "Academics" section, then navigate to "Special Education" or "Student Support Services." Here, you should find a directory of staff members, their roles, and contact information. Be aware that websites are not always up to date, so if you have trouble finding the information you need, contact the district directly to confirm current staff assignments. Typical places to look on the RCSD website include pages dedicated to the Committee on Special Education (CSE) or the Special Education Department. These pages often list key personnel and their contact details. If specific names are not listed, look for general contact information for the CSE office. Remember to document all your communication attempts, including the date, time, method of contact, and the person you spoke with (if applicable).

Key Roles to Know

Understanding the roles of key personnel within the Rochester City SD special education system is crucial for effective communication and advocacy. Here are some of the most important roles you'll encounter: * **Director of Special Education:** This individual oversees the entire special education program within the district. Contact the Director of Special Education when you have systemic concerns, policy-related questions, or if you've been unable to resolve issues at the school or CSE level. This person is responsible for ensuring that the district complies with federal and state special education laws. * **CSE Chairperson:** The CSE Chairperson is responsible for running Committee on Special Education (CSE) meetings and coordinating evaluations. They ensure that all required members are present, that the meeting is conducted according to regulations, and that the IEP is developed appropriately. Contact the CSE Chairperson to schedule meetings, request specific evaluations, or address concerns about the IEP process. * **Related Services Coordinator:** This role manages the scheduling and provision of related services such as speech therapy, occupational therapy (OT), and physical therapy (PT). If your child's IEP includes related services, the Related Services Coordinator is your point of contact for scheduling, service delivery concerns, or requests for changes to the service plan. * **School Psychologist:** The School Psychologist conducts psychological evaluations, participates in CSE meetings, and provides support to students and families. They play a key role in determining a student's eligibility for special education services and in developing appropriate IEP goals and accommodations. Contact the School Psychologist with questions about evaluations, behavioral interventions, or social-emotional support.

How to Submit Written Requests

In all special education matters, it is critical to document your requests and communications in writing. This creates a clear record of your interactions with the Rochester City SD and helps ensure that your concerns are addressed. Here's how to submit written requests effectively: Always put your requests in writing for the following: * **Evaluation Requests:** To formally request an initial evaluation or re-evaluation for special education services. * **Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) Requests:** If you disagree with the district's evaluation, you have the right to request an IEE at public expense under certain conditions. * **Record Requests:** To obtain copies of your child's educational records, including evaluations, IEPs, and progress reports. * **IEP Disagreements:** To formally document your disagreement with any aspect of your child's IEP. When submitting written requests, consider sending them via certified mail with return receipt requested. This provides proof that the district received your communication. Keep the tracking number and the return receipt for your records. Address your written requests to the Director of Special Education at the Rochester City SD. The mailing address is 131 West Broad Street, Rochester, NY 14614. You can also send a copy of your request to the CSE Chairperson and your child's school principal.

Finding Your Child's School-Level Team

Your child's school-level team is your primary point of contact for day-to-day questions and concerns about their IEP. This team typically includes: * **Case Manager:** The case manager is responsible for coordinating your child's special education services and ensuring that their IEP is implemented effectively. This person is your go-to contact for questions about IEP goals, accommodations, and progress monitoring. * **Special Education Teacher:** The special education teacher provides specialized instruction and support to your child based on their IEP goals. * **General Education Teacher:** If your child participates in general education classes, their general education teacher is also a key member of the team. They are responsible for implementing IEP accommodations and modifications in the general education setting. * **Building Principal:** The building principal is responsible for overseeing all aspects of the school's programs and services, including special education. They can connect you with the school psychologist or other relevant staff members. If you are unable to resolve an issue with your child's school-level team, escalate your concerns to the CSE office. Contact the CSE Chairperson to schedule a meeting or discuss your concerns. Remember to document all communication attempts and keep a record of any agreements or decisions made.

Escalation Path

If you are unable to resolve your concerns at the school or CSE level, it's important to know the proper escalation path. Here's a general outline of how to escalate issues within the Rochester City SD special education system: 1. **Building Team:** Start by addressing your concerns with your child's case manager, special education teacher, and general education teacher. 2. **CSE Chairperson:** If the building team is unable to resolve the issue, contact the CSE Chairperson to schedule a meeting or discuss your concerns. 3. **Director of Special Education:** If you are still unable to resolve the issue, contact the Director of Special Education. 4. **Superintendent:** As a last resort at the district level, you can contact the Superintendent of the Rochester City SD. 5. **NYSED State Complaint:** If you believe the district has violated federal or state special education laws, you can file a formal complaint with the New York State Education Department (NYSED). 6. **Impartial Hearing Officer:** If you disagree with the district's decisions regarding your child's special education services, you have the right to request an impartial hearing. In certain situations, it may be appropriate to skip levels in the escalation path. For example, if you have urgent concerns about your child's safety or if you believe they are being discriminated against, you should immediately contact the Director of Special Education or the Superintendent.

State and Regional Resources That Don't Change

Regardless of staff changes within the Rochester City SD, certain state and regional resources remain constant. These resources can provide valuable support and guidance as you navigate the special education system: * **NYSED Office of Special Education:** This office oversees special education programs and services throughout New York State. You can contact them at 518-473-2878 for general information or assistance. * **NYSED State Complaint:** You have the right to file a written complaint with NYSED if you believe the Rochester City SD has violated federal or state special education laws. The complaint must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. * **Special Education Quality Assurance (SEQA):** SEQA is a regional office of NYSED that monitors special education programs and services. Contact the relevant SEQA office for your region if you have concerns about the quality of special education services in the Rochester City SD. To find the correct SEQA office, search the NYSED website for "SEQA contact information" and filter by county. * **PTI (Parent Training and Information) Center:** PTI centers provide free information, training, and support to parents of children with disabilities. Search online for the PTI center serving the Rochester area. * **Disability Rights New York (DRNY):** DRNY is a non-profit organization that provides legal assistance and advocacy to people with disabilities in New York State. They can be reached via their website or by phone. Remember, you are not alone in navigating the special education system. These resources are available to help you advocate for your child's rights and ensure they receive the appropriate services and support. Always document your communications and keep a record of all relevant information.
