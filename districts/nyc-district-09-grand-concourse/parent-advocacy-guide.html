Understanding Your Rights Under IDEA

The Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) ensures that children with disabilities have access to a free appropriate public education (FAPE) tailored to their needs. Key components of IDEA that protect you and your child include:

  • Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE): Special education and related services are provided at public expense.
  • Individualized Education Program (IEP): A written plan developed by a team, including parents, outlining the child's needs, goals, and services.
  • Least Restrictive Environment (LRE): Children with disabilities should be educated with non-disabled children to the greatest extent possible.
  • Parental Rights (Procedural Safeguards): You have the right to participate in all meetings, examine records, and obtain an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) under specific circumstances.

The CSE Process in NYC District 09

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) in NYC District 09 is responsible for identifying and providing special education services. Here's a general overview:

  1. Referral: A child can be referred by a parent, teacher, or doctor. The referral should be written and detail specific concerns. Keep a copy!
  2. Evaluation: The CSE determines if an evaluation is needed. Evaluations assess all areas related to the suspected disability, which might include psychological, educational, or therapeutic assessments.
  3. CSE Meeting: The CSE meets to discuss evaluation results and determine eligibility for services. Parents are critical members of this team.
  4. IEP Development: If eligible, the CSE develops an IEP with measurable goals, services, and accommodations.

Preparing for a CSE Meeting

Effective advocacy starts with preparation. Consider these tips:

  • Review all documents: Thoroughly read your child's evaluations and any proposed IEP drafts before the meeting.
  • Document your concerns: Write down specific concerns and questions you have about your child's education.
  • Bring support: You can bring a friend, advocate, or therapist to the CSE meeting for support.
  • Ask questions: Don't hesitate to ask questions and seek clarification on anything you don't understand.

Frequently Asked Questions

NYC District 09 Grand Concourse parents ask

What is an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE)?

An IEE is an evaluation of your child conducted by a qualified professional who is not employed by the school district. If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you have the right to request an IEE, and NYC District 09 may be required to pay for it, or prove their evaluation was appropriate.

How do I request a CSE meeting in NYC District 09?

To request a CSE meeting, submit a written request to your child's school principal or the District 09 special education office. Clearly state the purpose of the meeting (e.g., annual review, IEP revision, eligibility determination) and include any supporting documentation that may be relevant.

What should I do if I disagree with the IEP?

If you disagree with the IEP developed by the CSE, you have several options. You can request another meeting to discuss your concerns, pursue mediation, or file a formal complaint with the New York State Education Department. Document your disagreements in writing and seek guidance from a special education advocate familiar with NYC District 09.

What are 'related services' in special education?

Related services are supports required to assist a child with a disability to benefit from special education. These can include speech therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, counseling services, transportation, and other supportive services as determined by the CSE in your child's IEP. In NYC District 09, these services are crucial for many students.
