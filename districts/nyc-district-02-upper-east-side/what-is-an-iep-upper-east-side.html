What Is an IEP in NYC District 02 - Tribeca / Greenwich Village?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services and supports your child needs to succeed in school. If you live in NYC District 02, which serves the vibrant neighborhoods of Tribeca and Greenwich Village, your child has the right to a free appropriate public education (FAPE) designed specifically for their unique learning needs.

For families in District 02, navigating the IEP process can feel overwhelming, especially when balancing the demands of city life with advocating for your child. Whether your child attends a local elementary school like PS 3 or PS 234, or you're considering specialized programs, understanding your rights and the available services is crucial for ensuring your child receives the support they deserve in one of Manhattan's most diverse educational communities.

The IEP Process in NYC District 02 - Tribeca / Greenwich Village

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in NYC District 02 follows the same citywide framework while serving a uniquely diverse community. District 02 serves families from various socioeconomic backgrounds, with both longtime residents and newcomers who may speak multiple languages at home, including Spanish, Chinese, and other languages reflecting the neighborhood's international character.

The process begins when you or your child's teacher suspects a disability that impacts learning. You'll submit a written request for evaluation to your child's school, which triggers a 60-day timeline for the CSE to complete assessments and hold an IEP meeting. District 02 schools work with the centralized CSE office to coordinate evaluations, which may include psychological, educational, speech-language, occupational therapy, or physical therapy assessments depending on your child's needs.

Once evaluations are complete, the CSE team meets to review results and determine if your child is eligible for special education services. If eligible, the team develops an IEP that includes annual goals, service recommendations, and placement decisions. In District 02, this might mean services within your neighborhood school, a specialized class, or potentially a District 75 program for children with more intensive needs.

Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

• Your child's general education teacher • A special education teacher or service provider • A school psychologist or district representative who can interpret evaluation results • A district representative who can authorize services and oversee implementation • Related service providers (speech therapist, occupational therapist, etc.) if applicable • You, the parent or guardian • Your child (when appropriate, typically age 15 and older) • Any additional members you or the school district invite

As a parent, you have the right to meaningfully participate in all decisions about your child's education and to invite advocates or other support people to attend meetings with you.

Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 02 - Tribeca / Greenwich Village

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), you have fundamental rights that protect your child's access to appropriate special education services. These include the right to request evaluations, participate in IEP meetings, receive prior written notice of any proposed changes, and access your child's educational records.

NYC provides additional protections through procedural safeguards, including the right to independent educational evaluations if you disagree with the district's assessment, the right to mediation services, and the right to an impartial due process hearing. You also have the right to receive documents in your preferred language and to have interpretation services during meetings.

If you disagree with the CSE's recommendations, you can request mediation through the NYC DOE or file for due process. The city also offers resolution sessions as an opportunity to resolve disputes before formal hearings.

Common IEP Services Available in NYC

Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) Classes : General education classrooms with both a general education and special education teacher serving students with and without disabilities together.

Special Education Teacher Support Services (SETSS) : Direct instruction from a special education teacher, either individual or small group, to address specific learning needs.

Speech-Language Therapy : Services to address communication disorders, including articulation, language processing, and social communication skills.

Occupational Therapy (OT) : Support for fine motor skills, sensory processing, and activities of daily living that impact school performance.

Physical Therapy (PT) : Services to address gross motor skills, mobility, and physical development needs.

Counseling Services : Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional needs that impact educational performance.

Assistive Technology : Devices and services that help students access the curriculum, from simple tools to complex communication systems.

Paraprofessional Support : A one-on-one or shared aide to provide additional assistance in academic or behavioral areas.

Next Steps for Parents

  1. Document your concerns by keeping detailed records of your child's academic performance, behaviors, and any communications with school staff about potential learning difficulties.

  2. Request an evaluation in writing to your child's principal or CSE office, clearly stating your concerns and why you believe your child may need special education services.

  3. Prepare for the CSE meeting by reviewing evaluation reports, writing down questions, and considering what services might help your child succeed.

  4. Participate actively in the IEP development process by sharing your knowledge of your child's strengths, needs, and what works at home.

  5. Follow up regularly with your child's teachers and service providers to monitor progress and ensure IEP implementation.
