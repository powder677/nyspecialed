Understanding the CSE in NYC District 17

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) in NYC District 17 is responsible for identifying students with disabilities, evaluating their needs, and ensuring they receive appropriate services. This process is guided by the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State regulations.

The CSE's goal is to create an Individualized Education Program (IEP) tailored to your child's unique needs. Parents are integral members of this committee and have a right to participate fully in all meetings and decisions.

Initiating the Special Education Process: The Referral

The special education process begins with a referral. Anyone can submit a referral if they suspect a child has a disability that affects their educational performance. This includes parents, teachers, doctors, or other professionals familiar with the child.

To initiate the process, submit a written referral to the school principal or the NYC District 17 CSE. Include any supporting documentation, such as:

  • Medical records
  • Previous evaluations
  • School reports

Key Steps in the CSE Process

  1. Initial CSE Meeting: The CSE will convene to determine if an individual evaluation is warranted. Parents are required members of the CSE.
  2. Individual Evaluation: If deemed necessary, the school district conducts a comprehensive evaluation at no cost to parents. The evaluation must assess all areas of suspected disability.
  3. Evaluation Report: Parents are entitled to receive a copy of the evaluation report prior to the CSE meeting discussing eligibility and placement.
  4. Eligibility Determination: The CSE will determine if your child meets the criteria for a disability under IDEA and New York State regulations.
  5. IEP Development: If eligible, the CSE will develop an Individualized Education Program (IEP) outlining your child's needs, goals, and services.
  6. Placement: Based on the IEP, the CSE will determine the appropriate placement for your child in the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).
  7. Annual Review: The IEP is reviewed at least annually to assess progress and make necessary adjustments.

Frequently Asked Questions

NYC District 17 Crown Heights parents ask

What is the role of the Committee on Special Education (CSE)?

The CSE is responsible for identifying, evaluating, and providing services for students with disabilities. They create Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) tailored to each child's unique needs. Parents are crucial members of the CSE team.

What if I disagree with the CSE's recommendations for my child in NYC District 17?

You have the right to disagree with the CSE's recommendations. You can request an independent educational evaluation (IEE) at public expense if you believe the school's evaluation was inadequate. You can also pursue mediation or file a complaint with the New York State Education Department.

How often is my child's IEP reviewed and updated?

Your child's IEP must be reviewed and updated at least once a year. However, you can request an IEP meeting at any time if you have concerns about your child's progress or if there are significant changes in their needs. Don't hesitate to reach out to the NYC District 17 CSE.

What is an Individualized Education Program (IEP)?

An IEP, or Individualized Education Program, is a legal document created for students with disabilities. It outlines a child's present levels of performance, measurable annual goals, and the specific special education services and supports they will receive to help them succeed in school. Parents are key contributors to the IEP process in NYC District 17.
