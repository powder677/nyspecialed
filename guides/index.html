New York State CSE Meeting Guide

Your voice is the most important one in the room. Navigate the Committee on Special Education with confidence.

Note: This guide is a helpful overview; it is not a substitute for legal advice.

What is a CSE Meeting?

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) determines eligibility for services and develops the Individualized Education Program (IEP).

  • Annual Review: A yearly "tune-up" for the IEP.
  • Initial Eligibility: The first meeting to see if a child qualifies.

Who Attends the Meeting?

Under NYS Part 200 regulations, you have a legal right to participate. Other members include:

  • District Representative: The person who can commit district resources.
  • School Psychologist: To interpret evaluation data.
  • Teachers: Both General and Special Education.

The Standard Agenda

  1. Introductions
  2. Review of Evaluations: Discussing new data or parent concerns.
  3. PLOP: Present Levels of Performance (The foundation of the IEP).
  4. SMART Goals: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, and Time-bound.
  5. Services and Placement: Determining the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).

Crucial Tips for NY Parents

  • Request Records: Ask for evaluations 5 business days before the meeting (NYS Law).
  • Bring an Advocate: You don't have to do this alone.
  • Trust Your Gut: You know your child better than any test score does.
