# What Is an IEP in NYC District 18 - Canarsie / Flatlands?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the specialized instruction and related services your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has a disability that affects their learning, an IEP ensures they receive free, appropriate public education tailored to their unique needs. This isn't just paperwork—it's your child's educational roadmap and your family's protection under federal and state law.

For families in Canarsie and Flatlands, understanding the IEP process is especially important given our community's diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds. Whether you're navigating this process in English, Spanish, Haitian Creole, or another language, you have the right to receive information and participate in meetings in your preferred language. Your child deserves every opportunity to thrive, and an effective IEP is often the key to unlocking their potential.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 18 - Canarsie / Flatlands

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in NYC District 18 follows these essential steps:

**1. Initial Referral:** You, your child's teacher, or school staff can request a special education evaluation. Submit your request in writing to your school's principal or district office.

**2. Evaluation:** The school has 60 school days to complete a comprehensive evaluation, which may include psychological, educational, speech-language, and other assessments as needed.

**3. CSE Meeting:** A team meets to review evaluation results and determine if your child is eligible for special education services. In District 18, these meetings often accommodate our community's multilingual needs with translation services.

**4. IEP Development:** If eligible, the CSE team creates an IEP with specific goals, services, and accommodations. This reflects both your child's needs and your family's cultural and linguistic preferences.

**5. Annual Review:** The IEP is reviewed and updated yearly, with additional meetings held as needed to address your child's changing needs.

District 18 serves families from diverse Caribbean, Latin American, and African communities, and the CSE process respects these cultural backgrounds while ensuring every child receives appropriate services.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

Required CSE team members include:

• Parents/guardians
• General education teacher
• Special education teacher
• School psychologist or other qualified evaluator
• District representative (administrator)
• Related services providers (if applicable)
• Your child (when appropriate)
• Any other individuals with special knowledge about your child

As a parent, you have the right to bring advocates, family members, or other supportive individuals to the meeting, and you can request translation services if needed.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 18 - Canarsie / Flatlands

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have important rights:

**Prior Written Notice:** The school must notify you before making any changes to your child's identification, evaluation, placement, or services.

**Procedural Safeguards:** You're entitled to a written explanation of all your rights, provided in your native language when possible.

**Independent Educational Evaluation:** If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an independent evaluation at public expense.

**Due Process Rights:** You can file complaints and request hearings if you disagree with the school's decisions.

**Language Access:** NYC DOE must provide interpretation and translation services for families who speak languages other than English, which is particularly important in our diverse District 18 community.

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

**Special Class (SC):** Small, specialized classrooms with lower student-to-teacher ratios for students needing intensive support.

**Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT):** General education classrooms with both a general and special education teacher working together.

**Related Services:** Including speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, and counseling services.

**Speech-Language Therapy:** Addresses communication challenges, articulation, language processing, and social communication skills.

**Occupational Therapy:** Helps with fine motor skills, sensory processing, and activities of daily living.

**Physical Therapy:** Supports gross motor development, mobility, and physical functioning.

**Counseling Services:** Provides emotional and behavioral support to help students succeed academically and socially.

**Assistive Technology:** Tools and devices that help students access curriculum and demonstrate their learning.

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document your concerns** by keeping written records of your child's challenges and any communications with school staff.

2. **Request an evaluation in writing** if you suspect your child needs special education services—don't wait for the school to suggest it.

3. **Gather supporting documents** including report cards, work samples, medical records, and any outside evaluations.

4. **Prepare for meetings** by reviewing your rights, writing down questions, and considering what services your child might need.

5. **Stay involved** by regularly communicating with teachers and monitoring your child's progress toward IEP goals.
