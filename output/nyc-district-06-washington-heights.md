# What Is an IEP in NYC District 06 - Washington Heights?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services and supports your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has a disability that impacts their learning, an IEP ensures they receive appropriate accommodations, modifications, and related services at no cost to your family. In NYC District 06 - Washington Heights, thousands of families rely on IEPs to help their children access the education they deserve.

For families in Washington Heights, understanding the IEP process is especially important given our community's rich diversity and the unique needs of our multilingual student population. Whether your family speaks Spanish, Arabic, Bengali, or any other language, you have the right to participate fully in your child's IEP process with interpretation services provided by the school district. The Committee on Special Education (CSE) in District 06 works with families to create individualized plans that respect cultural backgrounds while ensuring every child receives appropriate educational support.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 06 - Washington Heights

The IEP process in NYC District 06 follows the same citywide procedures mandated by federal and state law. Here's how it works for families in Washington Heights:

**Step 1: Referral** - You, your child's teacher, or school staff can request a special education evaluation. Given the diverse community in District 06, all communications and forms are available in your preferred language.

**Step 2: Evaluation** - The school conducts comprehensive assessments to determine if your child has a disability and needs special education services. Evaluations are conducted by qualified professionals who understand the linguistic and cultural diversity of our Washington Heights community.

**Step 3: Eligibility Determination** - The Committee on Special Education (CSE) reviews evaluation results to determine if your child qualifies for special education services under one of 13 disability categories.

**Step 4: IEP Development** - If eligible, the CSE team meets to develop your child's IEP, taking into account their individual needs, strengths, and the goals your family has for their education.

**Step 5: Placement Decision** - The team determines the least restrictive environment where your child can receive appropriate services, whether in general education classes with supports, specialized programs, or other settings.

**Step 6: Implementation and Review** - Services begin, and the IEP is reviewed annually to ensure it continues to meet your child's evolving needs.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team for your child's IEP meeting includes:

• Your child's general education teacher
• A special education teacher
• A school psychologist or district representative
• A related service provider (if applicable)
• You, the parent or guardian
• Your child (when appropriate, typically age 14+)
• Anyone else you or the school invites who has knowledge about your child

As a parent, you are an equal member of this team and have the right to meaningfully participate in all decisions about your child's education, including having an interpreter present if needed.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 06 - Washington Heights

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have significant rights:

• **Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE)** - Your child is entitled to special education services at no cost to your family
• **Least Restrictive Environment** - Your child should be educated with typical peers to the maximum extent appropriate
• **Prior Written Notice** - The school must inform you in writing before making any changes to your child's IEP
• **Consent Rights** - Your written consent is required before initial evaluations, reevaluations, and initial placement
• **Language Rights** - All IEP documents and meetings must be provided in your preferred language
• **Due Process Rights** - You can challenge school decisions through mediation, due process hearings, or complaints
• **Independent Educational Evaluation** - If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an independent evaluation at public expense

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

NYC District 06 schools can provide various services through your child's IEP:

• **Related Services** - Speech therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, counseling, and more
• **Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT)** - General and special education teachers working together in the same classroom
• **Special Class in a Community School** - Smaller classes with specialized instruction for students with similar needs
• **Resource Room Services** - Pull-out support for specific subjects or skills
• **Assistive Technology** - Devices and software to help your child access the curriculum
• **English Language Learning Support** - Additional services for multilingual learners with disabilities
• **Counseling Services** - Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional needs
• **Transition Services** - For students 15+ to prepare for post-secondary education and employment

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document your concerns** - Keep detailed records of your child's academic, behavioral, or developmental challenges
2. **Request an evaluation** - Submit a written request to your child's school principal or assistant principal
3. **Participate actively** - Attend all meetings, ask questions, and share your insights about your child's needs
4. **Review all documents** - Carefully read evaluation reports, IEPs, and any proposed changes before signing
5. **Know your timeline** - Schools have 60 school days from consent to complete evaluations and hold the initial IEP meeting
