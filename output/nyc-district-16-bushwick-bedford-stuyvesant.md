# What Is an IEP in NYC District 16 - Bushwick / Bedford-Stuyvesant?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services, accommodations, and supports your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has been identified with a disability that affects their learning, an IEP ensures they receive a free and appropriate public education (FAPE) tailored to their unique needs in NYC District 16.

For families in Bushwick and Bedford-Stuyvesant, understanding the IEP process is crucial for advocating for your child's educational rights. Our diverse community includes families who speak Spanish, Arabic, and other languages, and you have the right to receive all IEP information and participate in meetings in your preferred language. The IEP is your roadmap to ensuring your child gets the support they need to thrive academically and socially.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 16 - Bushwick / Bedford-Stuyvesant

The IEP process in NYC District 16 begins when your child is referred for special education services, either by you as the parent, their teacher, or another school professional. Here's how the Committee on Special Education (CSE) process works in our district:

1. **Initial Referral**: You or your child's school can request an evaluation if there are concerns about your child's academic progress or behavior.

2. **Consent and Evaluation**: The school must obtain your written consent before conducting any evaluations. These assessments look at your child's academic, social, emotional, and physical development.

3. **CSE Meeting**: Within 60 school days of consent, the Committee on Special Education will meet to review evaluation results and determine if your child is eligible for special education services.

4. **IEP Development**: If eligible, the CSE team will create an IEP that includes your child's present levels of performance, annual goals, and the services they'll receive.

5. **Implementation**: Your child's IEP must be implemented immediately once you consent to services.

6. **Annual Review**: The CSE meets at least once a year to review and update your child's IEP, ensuring it continues to meet their evolving needs.

District 16 serves a vibrant, multicultural community where many families are navigating the education system while balancing work and family responsibilities. The CSE team understands these challenges and works to make the process as accessible as possible for all families.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The Committee on Special Education includes the following required members:

- Your child's general education teacher
- Special education teacher
- School psychologist or special education evaluator  
- District representative (who can authorize services)
- Related service providers (speech therapist, occupational therapist, etc.)
- You, the parent or guardian

As a parent, you are an equal member of the CSE team and have the right to participate fully in all decisions about your child's education.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 16 - Bushwick / Bedford-Stuyvesant

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have important rights:

- **Language Rights**: All IEP documents and meetings must be provided in your native language or preferred mode of communication
- **Prior Written Notice**: The school must notify you in writing before making any changes to your child's special education services
- **Consent Rights**: Your written consent is required before initial evaluations and before your child receives special education services for the first time
- **Access to Records**: You can review and obtain copies of your child's educational records
- **Independent Evaluations**: If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an independent educational evaluation at public expense
- **Due Process Rights**: You can file a complaint or request a due process hearing if you disagree with the school's decisions
- **Mediation Services**: Free mediation is available to help resolve disputes between parents and schools

NYC also provides additional protections, including the right to have an advocate or attorney present at IEP meetings and the right to receive translation services.

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

- **Speech-Language Therapy**: Helps children with communication challenges, including speech articulation, language comprehension, and social communication skills
- **Occupational Therapy**: Supports fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living skills needed for school success
- **Physical Therapy**: Addresses gross motor skills, mobility, and physical development needs
- **Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT)**: General and special education teachers work together in the same classroom to support students with and without disabilities
- **Special Class**: Smaller class sizes with specialized instruction for students who need more intensive support
- **Related Services**: Including counseling, social work services, transportation, and assistive technology
- **Paraprofessional Support**: One-on-one or small group support to help students access the curriculum
- **Behavioral Intervention Services**: Structured support for students with significant behavioral needs

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document your concerns**: Keep detailed records of your child's academic challenges, behavioral issues, or developmental delays
2. **Request an evaluation in writing**: Submit a written request to your child's school principal or Committee on Special Education
3. **Prepare for meetings**: Review your child's current performance, gather questions, and consider bringing an advocate or support person
4. **Stay involved**: Attend all IEP meetings, ask questions, and maintain regular communication with your child's teachers
5. **Know your timeline**: Understand that evaluations must be completed within 60 school days and IEP meetings must be held at least annually
