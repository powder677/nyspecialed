# What Is an IEP in NYC District 12 - Wakefield / Williamsbridge?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the specialized instruction and support services your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has a disability that impacts their learning, an IEP ensures they receive a free appropriate public education (FAPE) tailored to their unique needs in NYC District 12's Wakefield and Williamsbridge communities.

For families in District 12, where many students come from diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds, understanding the IEP process is crucial for ensuring your child receives the support they deserve. Whether your family speaks English, Spanish, or another language, you have the right to participate meaningfully in your child's education planning and receive all documents and meetings in your preferred language.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 12 - Wakefield / Williamsbridge

The IEP process in NYC District 12 follows the citywide Committee on Special Education (CSE) framework, with considerations for the unique needs of the Wakefield and Williamsbridge communities. Here's how it works:

1. **Referral**: Teachers, parents, or other professionals can request an evaluation if they suspect a child has a disability affecting their education.

2. **Initial Evaluation**: The district has 60 calendar days to complete a comprehensive evaluation, including academic, psychological, and any necessary related service assessments.

3. **Eligibility Determination**: The CSE team reviews evaluation results to determine if your child qualifies for special education services under one of 13 disability categories.

4. **IEP Development**: If eligible, the CSE creates an IEP within 60 calendar days of consent for evaluation, including annual goals, service recommendations, and placement decisions.

5. **Implementation**: Your child begins receiving services as outlined in the IEP, with progress monitoring throughout the year.

6. **Annual Review**: The IEP team meets at least once per year to review progress and update the program as needed.

In District 12's diverse communities, CSE teams are experienced working with multilingual families and understand the cultural factors that may impact a child's educational experience.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

Required CSE team members include:

• Parents or guardians
• Regular education teacher (if child participates in general education)
• Special education teacher or provider
• School psychologist or other qualified professional
• District representative (typically an administrator)
• Related service providers (as appropriate)
• Student (when 15 or older, or younger if appropriate)

As a parent, you are an equal member of this team with the right to participate in all decisions about your child's education.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 12 - Wakefield / Williamsbridge

Under federal IDEA law and New York State Education Law, you have significant rights:

• **Prior Written Notice**: Receive written notice before any changes to your child's IEP or placement
• **Native Language Rights**: Request all meetings, documents, and communications in your preferred language
• **Independent Educational Evaluation**: Obtain an evaluation at public expense if you disagree with the district's assessment
• **Due Process Rights**: File complaints or request impartial hearings if disputes arise
• **Stay-Put Protection**: Keep your child in their current placement during disputes
• **Access to Records**: Review all educational records related to your child

NYC also provides additional protections, including the right to have a parent advocate accompany you to meetings and specific timelines for service implementation.

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

**Special Education Teacher Support Services (SETSS)**: Direct instruction in a small group or one-on-one setting to address academic needs.

**Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT)**: General and special education teachers work together in inclusive classrooms.

**Speech-Language Therapy**: Services to address communication, language, and speech difficulties.

**Occupational Therapy (OT)**: Support for fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living activities.

**Physical Therapy (PT)**: Services to improve gross motor skills, mobility, and physical development.

**Counseling Services**: Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional needs.

**Assistive Technology**: Devices and services to help students access the curriculum and communicate effectively.

**Paraprofessional Support**: One-on-one or small group aide support for students who need additional assistance.

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document Your Concerns**: Keep detailed records of your child's academic, behavioral, or developmental challenges, including specific examples and dates.

2. **Request an Evaluation in Writing**: Submit a formal written request to your school principal or CSE chairperson for a comprehensive evaluation.

3. **Gather Outside Documentation**: Collect any medical reports, private evaluations, or therapy records that support your concerns.

4. **Know Your Timeline**: Understand that the district has 60 calendar days from your consent to complete the evaluation.

5. **Prepare for Meetings**: Review all evaluation reports before IEP meetings and prepare questions about recommended services and goals.
