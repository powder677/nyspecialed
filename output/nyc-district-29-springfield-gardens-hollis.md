# What Is an IEP in NYC District 29 - Springfield Gardens / Hollis?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that ensures your child receives the special education services they need to succeed in school. In NYC District 29, which serves the diverse communities of Springfield Gardens, Hollis, St. Albans, Queens Village, and surrounding neighborhoods, families have the right to request an IEP evaluation if they believe their child may have a disability that affects their learning.

For families in District 29's multicultural community—where many households speak multiple languages including English, Spanish, Hindi, Urdu, and Caribbean Creole—understanding your child's educational rights is crucial. An IEP provides your child with personalized support, from specialized instruction to related services like speech therapy, all designed to help them access the general education curriculum alongside their peers.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 29 - Springfield Gardens / Hollis

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in District 29 follows NYC DOE guidelines while serving families from diverse backgrounds. Here's how it works:

1. **Referral**: Parents, teachers, or other professionals can request an evaluation. Many District 29 families appreciate that interpretation services are available during this process.

2. **Initial Evaluation**: The CSE has 60 school days to complete evaluations, which may include psychological, educational, speech-language, occupational therapy, or physical therapy assessments.

3. **Eligibility Determination**: The CSE team reviews evaluation results to determine if your child has a disability and needs special education services.

4. **IEP Development**: If eligible, the team creates an IEP tailored to your child's unique needs, considering the rich cultural context of District 29's community.

5. **Annual Reviews**: IEPs are reviewed yearly, with input from parents who understand their child's needs both at school and in the community.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes:
• A special education teacher
• A general education teacher (if your child participates in general education)
• A school psychologist or other qualified evaluator
• A district representative who can authorize services
• Related service providers (if applicable)
• **You, the parent** - your input is essential and legally required

As a parent in District 29, you have the right to bring advocates, interpreters, or other support people to your IEP meeting.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 29 - Springfield Gardens / Hollis

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have significant rights:

• **Prior Written Notice**: The school must inform you in writing before making any changes to your child's IEP
• **Consent Rights**: Your written consent is required for initial evaluations and services
• **Language Access**: All IEP documents and meetings must be provided in your preferred language
• **Procedural Safeguards**: You can request independent evaluations, mediation, or due process hearings
• **Least Restrictive Environment**: Your child should be educated with non-disabled peers to the maximum extent appropriate
• **Access to Records**: You can review your child's educational records at any time

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

District 29 schools can provide various special education services:

• **Special Education Teacher Support Services (SETSS)**: Direct instruction in a small group or one-on-one setting
• **Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) Classes**: Two teachers (general and special education) co-teach in the same classroom
• **Speech-Language Therapy**: Addresses communication, language, and speech disorders
• **Occupational Therapy**: Helps with fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living activities
• **Physical Therapy**: Supports gross motor skills and mobility
• **Counseling Services**: Provides emotional and behavioral support
• **Related Services**: Including assistive technology, transportation, and nursing services
• **12:1+1 Special Classes**: Smaller class sizes with additional paraprofessional support

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document your concerns** about your child's academic, social, or behavioral challenges in writing.

2. **Request an evaluation** by submitting a written request to your child's school principal and copying the District 29 CSE office.

3. **Gather supporting information** such as report cards, teacher notes, medical records, or outside evaluations.

4. **Stay involved** throughout the evaluation process and prepare questions for the IEP meeting.

5. **Know your timeline** - the CSE has 60 school days from consent to complete the evaluation and hold an IEP meeting if your child is found eligible.
