# What Is an IEP in NYC District 08 - Hunts Point / Morrisania?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that ensures your child with disabilities receives the specialized instruction and services they need to succeed in school. Every child with an IEP has unique learning needs, and this plan outlines exactly how NYC District 08 schools will support your child's academic, social, and emotional growth.

For families in Hunts Point and Morrisania, understanding your IEP rights is especially important. Our community deserves quality special education services, and the IEP process is your pathway to ensuring your child gets the support they need to thrive. Whether your child attends school on Southern Boulevard, in the Hub area, or anywhere else in District 08, you have the power to advocate for appropriate services and accommodations.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 08 - Hunts Point / Morrisania

The IEP process in District 08 begins when you or your child's teacher suspects your child may need special education services. Here's how it works:

1. **Initial Referral**: You can request an evaluation by contacting your child's school or calling the District 08 office directly. Don't let language barriers stop you—schools must provide interpretation services in Spanish and other languages spoken in our community.

2. **Comprehensive Evaluation**: A team of specialists will assess your child's academic, social, and developmental needs. This includes educational testing, psychological evaluation, and any necessary medical assessments.

3. **Committee on Special Education (CSE) Meeting**: If your child qualifies for special education, the CSE team will meet to develop the IEP. This meeting brings together educators, specialists, and most importantly, you as the parent.

4. **IEP Development**: The team creates a detailed plan outlining your child's goals, the services they'll receive, and where those services will be provided. Many District 08 families benefit from Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) classes, speech therapy, and other supportive services.

5. **Implementation and Monitoring**: Once you agree to the IEP, the school begins providing services. The plan is reviewed annually, with progress monitored throughout the year.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

Your child's CSE team includes these required members:

• A District 08 administrator or special education supervisor
• Your child's general education teacher
• A special education teacher
• A school psychologist or social worker
• Related service providers (speech therapist, occupational therapist, etc.)
• You, the parent—the most important member of the team

As a parent in NYC, you have the right to bring an advocate, translator, or other support person to any IEP meeting.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 08 - Hunts Point / Morrisania

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have powerful rights:

• **Prior Written Notice**: The school must inform you in writing before making any changes to your child's IEP or placement
• **Consent Rights**: Your permission is required before initial evaluations and before your child receives special education services for the first time
• **Language Rights**: All IEP documents and meetings must be provided in your native language
• **Procedural Safeguards**: You have the right to request mediation, file complaints, or request due process hearings if you disagree with the school's decisions
• **Access to Records**: You can review all of your child's educational records
• **Independent Educational Evaluation**: If you disagree with the school's evaluation, you can request an independent assessment at public expense

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

District 08 schools can provide these special education services:

• **Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT)**: General and special education teachers work together in the same classroom
• **Speech-Language Therapy**: Helps children with communication, language, and speech difficulties
• **Occupational Therapy (OT)**: Supports fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living skills
• **Physical Therapy (PT)**: Addresses gross motor skills, mobility, and physical development
• **Special Class**: Smaller, specialized classrooms for students who need more intensive support
• **SETSS (Special Education Teacher Support Services)**: One-on-one or small group instruction
• **Counseling Services**: Individual or group counseling to support social-emotional development
• **Assistive Technology**: Devices and software that help students access the curriculum

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document Your Concerns**: Write down specific examples of your child's struggles in school, including academic challenges, behavioral issues, or social difficulties.

2. **Request an Evaluation**: Submit a written request to your child's school principal or contact the District 08 office. Keep copies of all communications.

3. **Gather Information**: Collect report cards, teacher comments, work samples, and any outside evaluations or medical records related to your child's needs.

4. **Prepare for Meetings**: Research your child's school, understand their current programs, and think about what services might help your child succeed.

5. **Stay Involved**: Attend all meetings, ask questions, and don't be afraid to advocate for your child's needs.
