# What Is an IEP in NYC District 19 - East New York / Starrett City?

An Individualized Education Program (IEP) is a legally binding document that outlines the special education services and supports your child needs to succeed in school. If your child has a disability that affects their learning, an IEP ensures they receive free, appropriate public education tailored to their unique needs. This vital document serves as your child's educational roadmap, detailing their goals, services, accommodations, and the specialized instruction they'll receive.

For families in East New York and Starrett City, understanding the IEP process is especially important given our community's rich diversity and the unique challenges many families face. Whether you're a Spanish-speaking parent, recently arrived immigrant, or long-time Brooklyn resident, you have the same fundamental rights to advocate for your child's education. The NYC Department of Education must provide translation services and ensure all families can fully participate in their child's special education journey, regardless of language barriers or economic circumstances.

## The IEP Process in NYC District 19 - East New York / Starrett City

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) process in NYC District 19 follows the same federal and state guidelines as all NYC districts, but understanding your local community context can help you navigate the system more effectively. East New York and Starrett City are home to vibrant Caribbean, Latino, African American, and other immigrant communities, with families speaking dozens of languages including Spanish, Haitian Creole, Arabic, and various African languages.

The CSE process begins when you or your child's teacher suspects your child may need special education services. Here's how it works in District 19:

1. **Referral**: You, your child's teacher, or another school professional can request an evaluation
2. **Initial Evaluation**: The school has 60 school days to complete a comprehensive evaluation
3. **CSE Meeting**: If your child is found eligible, the CSE team meets to develop the IEP
4. **IEP Implementation**: Services begin within 10 school days of the signed IEP
5. **Annual Review**: The CSE meets at least once yearly to review and update the IEP

The NYC DOE must provide interpreters for all meetings and translate documents into your home language. Don't let language barriers prevent you from being your child's strongest advocate.

## Who Attends the IEP Meeting

The CSE team includes these required members:

• **General education teacher** (if your child participates in general education)
• **Special education teacher** or special education provider
• **School psychologist or other qualified professional** who can interpret evaluation results
• **School district representative** (administrator who can commit district resources)
• **Parent or guardian** (that's you!)
• **Your child** (when appropriate, typically age 14+)
• **Related service providers** (speech therapist, occupational therapist, etc., if applicable)
• **Additional specialists** as needed for your child's specific needs

As a parent, you have the right to invite anyone you choose to support you at the meeting, including advocates, family members, or professionals who know your child.

## Your Rights as a Parent in NYC District 19 - East New York / Starrett City

Under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) and New York State Education Law, you have extensive rights to ensure your child receives appropriate special education services:

**Your fundamental rights include:**
- The right to have all meetings and documents translated into your home language
- The right to request an evaluation if you suspect your child has a disability
- The right to participate in all IEP meetings and decisions
- The right to review all educational records and evaluation reports
- The right to obtain an independent educational evaluation at public expense if you disagree with the school's evaluation
- The right to prior written notice before the school changes your child's program
- The right to dispute resolution through mediation or due process hearings

**NYC-specific protections include:**
- Access to the Office of Safety and Youth Development for support
- The right to file complaints with the NYC DOE's Office of Special Education Initiatives
- Protection under the NYC Human Rights Law against discrimination
- Access to translation and interpretation services at no cost to you

## Common IEP Services Available in NYC

NYC District 19 schools can provide a wide range of special education services based on your child's needs:

• **Integrated Co-Teaching (ICT) Classes**: General and special education teachers work together to serve students with and without disabilities in the same classroom

• **Special Class in a Community School (SC)**: Smaller classes with specialized instruction for students who need more intensive support

• **Related Services**: Including speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, physical therapy, counseling, and vision or hearing services

• **Speech-Language Therapy**: Individual or group therapy to address communication difficulties, language delays, and speech articulation issues

• **Occupational Therapy**: Support for fine motor skills, sensory processing, and daily living skills needed for school success

• **Counseling Services**: Individual or group counseling to address social-emotional needs that impact learning

• **Assistive Technology**: Devices and services that help students access the curriculum and demonstrate their knowledge

• **Paraprofessional Support**: One-on-one or small group assistance to help students access instruction and participate in school activities

## Next Steps for Parents

1. **Document your concerns**: Keep detailed records of your child's academic challenges, behavioral issues, or developmental concerns you've observed at home or school.

2. **Request an evaluation in writing**: Submit a written request to your child's school principal and copy the District 19 superintendent. The school must respond within 10 school days.

3. **Gather supporting information**: Collect any medical records, previous evaluations, report cards, and work samples that demonstrate your child's needs.

4. **Prepare for meetings**: Write down questions in advance, bring a support person if helpful, and don't hesitate to ask for clarification during meetings.

5. **Know your timeline**: Understand that evaluations must be completed within 60 school days, and IEP services must begin within 10 school days of a signed IEP.
