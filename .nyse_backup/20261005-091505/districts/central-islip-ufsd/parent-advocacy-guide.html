We understand that navigating the special education system can be overwhelming. This guide provides information to help parents of children with disabilities in the Central Islip Union Free School District (UFSD) understand their rights and advocate effectively within the New York State Committee on Special Education (CSE) process. By understanding your role and the steps involved, you can ensure your child receives the appropriate support and services.

Understanding the CSE in Central Islip UFSD

The Committee on Special Education (CSE) is responsible for identifying students with disabilities, evaluating their needs, and developing Individualized Education Programs (IEPs) in Central Islip UFSD. The CSE team typically includes:

  • Parent(s) of the student
  • A regular education teacher (if the student participates in regular education)
  • A special education teacher or provider
  • A school psychologist
  • A district representative qualified in special education
  • An individual who can interpret evaluation results
  • Other knowledgeable individuals, including related service personnel
  • The student (when appropriate)

Parent Tip: You have the right to invite anyone you believe can contribute to the CSE meeting, such as a private therapist or advocate.

Initiating the Special Education Process

The special education process begins with a referral, which can be made by parents, teachers, doctors, or other professionals. The referral should be submitted in writing to the school principal or the district's special education office. Be sure to retain a copy for your records.

If the CSE determines an evaluation is warranted, parental consent is required. The evaluation will assess your child's strengths and weaknesses across various developmental areas. The evaluation must utilize a variety of assessment tools and strategies to gather functional, developmental, and academic information about your child.

IEP Development and Implementation

Following the evaluation, the CSE will meet to discuss the results and determine eligibility for special education services. If eligible, the CSE will develop an Individualized Education Program (IEP). The IEP is a legally binding document outlining your child's specific educational needs, goals, and the services and supports that will be provided. The IEP must include measurable annual goals.

The school district is responsible for implementing the IEP as written. Regular progress monitoring is crucial to ensuring your child is making adequate progress. The IEP must be reviewed at least annually, or more frequently if needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Central Islip UFSD parents ask

How do I request a special education evaluation for my child in Central Islip UFSD?

To request an evaluation, submit a written referral to your child's school principal or the Central Islip UFSD special education office. Be sure to include your concerns and any relevant information about your child's difficulties. Keep a copy of the referral for your records.

What are my rights as a parent during the CSE process in NY?

As a parent, you have the right to participate in all CSE meetings, review your child's records, and request an independent educational evaluation (IEE) if you disagree with the school's evaluation. You also have the right to advocate for your child's needs and ensure they receive a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE).

What should I do if I disagree with my child's IEP in Central Islip?

If you disagree with the IEP, you have several options. You can request another CSE meeting to discuss your concerns, participate in mediation, or file a complaint with the New York State Education Department. It's crucial to document your concerns and seek support from a special education advocate if needed. Central Islip UFSD is required to follow dispute resolution procedures.

What is included in an IEP?

An IEP includes information about a student's present levels of academic achievement and functional performance, measurable annual goals, special education and related services, accommodations and modifications, and how progress will be measured. It is a blueprint for how the Central Islip UFSD will provide a Free and Appropriate Public Education to your child.
